WHY IT MATTERS, GENTLEMEN

Your razor is doing more to your skin than you think.

Up to 83%Ingrown hairs & razor bumps

A close shave leaves a sharp tip that can curl back into the skin. Studies put razor bumps at 45–83% of Black men who shave regularly. Exfoliation clears the dead skin that traps those hairs so they grow out, not in.

Shave shadowDark marks from friction

Daily blade friction and the bumps it causes leave behind dark patches, called post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, most common in medium to deep skin. Gentle exfoliation and brightening treatments fade them over time.

~28 daysWhy exfoliating matters

Young skin renews itself roughly every four weeks. That cycle slows with age, so dead cells pile up — skin looks dull, feels rough, and pores clog. Exfoliation picks up where your skin slows down.

Out of reachYour back

The back is packed with oil glands, which makes it prone to clogged pores and breakouts — and it's the one spot you can't treat yourself. A Bacial or back wax handles what you can't reach.

AND FOR MY LADIES

You pour into everyone else. Let your skin be next.

MicrodermabrasionFresh skin, on the surface

A gentle mechanical exfoliation that lifts the outermost layer of dead cells. It softens rough texture and dullness, helps fade uneven tone and the look of fine lines, and lets your serums and moisturizers absorb better. No downtime — you walk out glowing.

ConsistencySkin changes with you

Hormones, stress, sun and time all show up on your skin — breakouts, dark spots, dryness, ingrown hairs after shaving or waxing. A regular facial every four to six weeks keeps up with your skin's renewal cycle instead of chasing problems after they start.

Razor bump figures: Perry PK et al., “Defining pseudofolliculitis barbae in 2001,” Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology, 2002. Educational information, not medical advice.
